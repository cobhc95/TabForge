# Effects and expression

Effects are the marks that tell the player how to play a note. A hammer-on, a bend, a palm mute and a loud accent are all effects. They sit on a note the way a pencil mark sits on a page: they tell the player what to do, and some of them also change the sound.

This chapter explains every effect in the **Effects** group, what each one does, when you would use it, and how to add it step by step. It also covers dynamics, accents, fades, strokes and the words you can put above a beat.

You do not need to learn all of them at once. Start with palm mute, hammer-on and vibrato. Add the others when a song calls for them.

## Where effects live

You can reach every effect in three places. The same effect does the same thing in each place.

- **The Effects group of the Tools panel.** This is a set of buttons. Hover a button to read its name and its key.
- **The Effects menu** in the menu bar. It lists every effect, with submenus for the ones that have several kinds.
- **The right-click menu on a note.** Right-click the note, then choose **Effects**.

Most effects also have one letter key. The keys are listed in each section below.

Tip: If you are not sure what a button does, hover over it and wait for the tooltip. The tooltip shows the name and the key.

## How an effect works

An effect is a switch on a note. It works the same way every time.

1. Put the cursor on a note, or select several beats.
2. Use the effect, with its key, the button, or the menu.
3. The effect turns on. The score shows its mark.
4. Use the same effect again on the same note, and it turns off.

If you select a stretch of beats first, the effect applies to every note in the selection at once. Use it once to mark all of them. Use it once more to remove the mark from all of them.

Tip: Select a range first and apply a technique once. Every note in the range is marked. One more press removes the mark from all of them.

Tip: With the cursor on an empty beat, pressing an effect key writes a new note that carries the effect. The note uses the current note length and sits on the cursor's string. A ghost note starts at fret 5. Most other effects start at fret 0, so you can add the mark without typing a fret first.

## Muting and sustain

These effects change how a note sounds, or how long it rings.

- **Palm mute** (`P`). Rest the side of your picking hand on the strings near the bridge. The notes sound short and dull, which is the chugging sound of many rock and metal riffs. The score shows a **P.M.** label with a dashed line over the notes. Use it when you want a tight, muted rhythm.
- **Let ring** (`I`). The note keeps sounding over the notes that follow. The score shows a line under the notes. Use it for open chords that should keep ringing.
- **Dead note** (`X`). A muted, percussive click with no clear pitch. The score draws an X on the fret. Use it for a percussive scratch.
- **Ghost note** (`O`). A very soft note, drawn with the fret in brackets. Use it for a quiet filler note between louder ones.
- **Staccato** (`Shift+1`). The note is played short, and a dot marks it. Use it when you want a crisp, detached note.
- **Tenuto** (`Shift+-`). The note is held for its full length, and a dash marks it. It is a mark only, so it does not change the sound. Use it to show the player to hold the note.
- **Accent** (`;`). Each press cycles through no accent, an accent and a heavy accent. An accent makes the note louder. Use it to push a beat forward.

Note: Keys are written as they appear on the keyboard. `Shift+1` means hold `Shift` and press the `1` key. `Shift+-` means hold `Shift` and press the minus key.

## Joining notes

These effects connect one note to the next.

- **Hammer-on / pull-off** (`H`). The second note sounds without being picked again. You hammer a finger down on a fret, or pull a finger off a string. The score draws a curved line over the notes. To mark a run, select the notes and press `H` once. A run is two or more notes in a row on one string.
- **Legato slide** (`S`). The finger slides along the string from this note to the next note on the same string, and the next note is not picked. The score draws a line between the two frets.
- **Shift slide**. A slide where the next note is picked again. It has no key. Choose **Effects > Slide > Shift slide** from the menu, or find it in the **Slide** submenu.

A tie is different. It joins two notes of the same pitch into one long sound, and it is the `L` key. See the chapter on writing notes.

Tip: To learn a hammer-on, write a run of three notes on one string, select them, and press `H`. Then play it back with `Space`.

## Bends, vibrato and the tremolo bar

These effects move the pitch of a note while it sounds.

- **Bend** (`B`). The string is pushed sideways and the note rises. The score shows a bend curve over the fret number. The bend editor draws the curve on a grid. Its presets include Bend/Release and Pre-Bend.
- **Vibrato** (`V`). A steady wobble in pitch, shown by a wavy line. Press `V` again on the same note to remove it.
- **Wide vibrato**. A bigger wobble. Choose it from the **Effects** menu, under **Vibrato**.
- **Vibrato settings**. Opens settings for the vibrato on the selected notes: normal or wide, and how deep and fast it wobbles. Use it when the default vibrato is too small or too big.
- **Tremolo bar** (`W`). The whammy lever dips the pitch. The score shows a whammy diagram. The tremolo bar editor draws the dip, and its presets include Dip and Dive.

To use an editor: press the key, or choose the item in the menu. The editor window opens. Draw or pick the shape, then close the window. The curve stays on the note.

Note: A bend or tremolo bar curve you draw is saved with the song, so it plays back the same way next time.

## Trills, grace notes and harmonics

These effects add quick notes or a different kind of tone.

- **Trill** (`N`). The note flicks quickly between itself and a note above it. The trill editor sets the fret for the trill and its speed. Use it for a fast alternating ornament.
- **Grace note** (`G`). Opens the grace note editor for the note. In the editor you set the fret, the position, the duration, the dynamic and the transition. Use it for a short ornament played just before the main note.
- **Natural harmonic** (`Y`). A bell-like ringing note made by touching the string lightly over a fret instead of pressing it down. Press `Y` to mark a natural harmonic. The harmonic editor chooses the kind and its fret. If the note already has a harmonic, `Y` removes it.
- **Harmonic editor**. Opens the editor to pick the harmonic type, such as Natural, Artificial, Tapped, Pinch or Semi, and its fret where one is needed.

Note: If you open a song that contains an artificial, pinch, tapped or semi harmonic, TabForge shows and plays it. You can add the natural harmonic yourself with `Y`.

## Palm mute, strokes and picking

These effects describe how the picking hand moves.

- **Brush down** and **Brush up**. The chord is strummed with a sweep across the strings. In the score, each stroke has an arrow. Use them for strummed chords.
- **Arpeggio down** and **Arpeggio up**. The notes of a chord are played one after another instead of together. Use them for a spread-out chord.
- **Pickstroke down** and **Pickstroke up**. Down and up picking marks for single notes. You find them in the **Beat** group of the **Tools** panel.
- **Tremolo picking**. Very fast repeated picking, drawn as slashes through the stem. Use it for a shimmering, buzzing note.
- **Tapping**, **Slap** and **Pop**. Right-hand tapping on the fretboard, and two bass techniques. These are marks only.

Strokes and tapping have no default keys. You can give them keys in **Options > Preferences > Shortcuts** if you want them.

Tip: Brush and arpeggio are in the **Effects** menu, and the pickstroke buttons are in the **Beat** group. Look in both places when you cannot find one.

## Dynamics

Dynamics say how loud a note is, from very soft to very loud. They run in this order: `ppp`, `pp`, `p`, `mp`, `mf`, `f`, `ff`, `fff`. The letters stand for the Italian words for soft and loud. `p` means soft, and `f` means loud.

- With notes selected, a dynamic sets how loud those notes are.
- With the cursor on an empty beat, a dynamic sets the loudness of the next notes you write.

The dynamic appears under the staff, at the first note it applies to. You find the buttons in the **Dynamic** group of the **Tools** panel. Dynamics have no default keys, so give them keys in **Options > Preferences > Shortcuts** if you want them.

## Accents and fades

These effects change the loudness of one note, or make the sound swell.

- **Accent** (`;`). Makes one note louder, cycling through accent and heavy accent as you press it.
- **Heavy accent**. The strongest accent, from the **Effects** group.
- **Fade in** (`Shift+,`). The sound swells up over the note. A widening hairpin marks it.
- **Fade out** (`Shift+.`). The sound swells down. A narrowing hairpin marks it.

Use a fade in to bring a note in softly, and a fade out to let it die away.

## Words and chords on the score

A chord name and a short text can sit above a beat. Neither is played. They are labels for the reader.

- **Chord name** (`A`). A window named **Chord name** opens. Type a name such as `Am` or `G7`, then click **OK**. TabForge prints names only. It does not draw chord diagrams.
- **Beat text** (`T`). A window named **Beat text** opens. Type a short word such as `chorus`, then click **OK**.
- **Chord finder**. Choose **Tools > Chord finder…**, pick a root and a type, click **Show** to see the notes, and click **Insert name** to place the name on the cursor beat.

Use chord names to help the player see the chords. Use beat text to mark a part of the song.

## Which marks change the sound

Most effects change what you hear. Some only change what you see.

- These change the sound: palm mute, let ring, dead note, ghost note, staccato, accents, fades, hammer-ons, slides, bends, vibrato, the tremolo bar, trills and harmonics.
- These are marks only: tenuto, tapping, pick strokes, chord names and beat text. They appear in the score but do not change the playback.

If you want the sound to match a mark, listen with `Space` after you add it.

## Common questions

Question: How do I remove an effect from a note?
Answer: Select the note, then use the same effect again. The effect is a switch, so one more press removes it.

Question: Why did my effect appear on a note I did not click?
Answer: You had a selection of beats active. The effect applies to every note in the selection. Press `Ctrl+Z` to undo, then click one note and try again.

Question: What is the difference between a palm mute and a dead note?
Answer: A palm mute makes the notes short and dull but still pitched. A dead note is a muted click with no clear pitch, drawn as an X.

Question: Which effects change the sound, and which only mark the score?
Answer: Most change the sound. Tenuto, tapping, pick strokes, chord names and beat text only mark the score.

Question: Can I give effects keys of my own?
Answer: Yes. Open **Options > Preferences > Shortcuts** and set a key for any command. Some effects, such as the strokes and dynamics, have no default key.

## Troubleshooting

Problem: I pressed an effect key and nothing appeared.
Fix: Click a note first, or click an empty beat on a string. An effect needs a note or a cursor position to act on.

Problem: I pressed a letter key and it typed a letter into a box.
Fix: The focus is inside a text box, such as the title or lyrics box. Click the score first, so the keys act as effects again.

Problem: The effect went on every note in my selection, and I only wanted one.
Fix: Press `Ctrl+Z` to undo. Then click the one note you want, with no selection, and use the effect again.

Problem: The palm mute mark is in the wrong place.
Fix: The mark follows the beat where you put it. Undo with `Ctrl+Z`, move the cursor to the correct beat, and apply the palm mute again.

Problem: The bend looks different from what I drew.
Fix: Open the bend editor again and check the curve. Choose a preset, such as Bend/Release, if you want a simple shape.

Problem: The tremolo bar sounds different from what I drew.
Fix: Open the tremolo bar editor again. Check the dip and dive shape, or pick a preset such as Dip or Dive.

Problem: I cannot find an effect in the menus.
Fix: Open the **Effects** menu. Use the **Effects** group in the **Tools** panel, and hover each button to read its name.

Problem: I added a hammer-on, but I cannot hear a difference.
Fix: Hammer-ons only sound different when the second note is played without a pick. Make sure the notes are on one string, with the mark over them, and play with `Space` to hear it.

Problem: The note I marked went quiet or loud and I did not mean it.
Fix: A dynamic or an accent may be on that note. Select the note and check its dynamic in the **Dynamic** group. Press the accent key again to cycle it back, or undo with `Ctrl+Z`.

Problem: The fade sounds like it swells up when I wanted it to fade away.
Fix: Fade in and fade out are separate. Use `Shift+,` for fade in and `Shift+.` for fade out, then check the hairpin shape.

Problem: My chord name or text does not appear where I wanted it.
Fix: Chord names and beat text sit above the beat under the cursor. Move the cursor to the right beat, and add the label again.
