# Rhythm and layout

This chapter covers how the notes are timed and how the page looks. The **Rhythm** panel holds tools for tuplets, ties and the sounding pitch of a note. The **Layout** panel holds tools for voices, line breaks, beams and stems. Together with zoom, the view options, the tempo box and the playback speed, they control how the score reads on screen and how fast the song plays.

You do not need to change these settings to write a song. Come back to them when a bar looks wrong, when the score is hard to read, or when you want a particular look on the page.

## Open the Rhythm and Layout panels

Both panels are tool pages in the side panel, next to **Tools** and **Structure**.

1. Find the **Rhythm** tab or the **Layout** tab in the side panel.
2. If a tab is missing, choose **View > Panels** and tick **Rhythm** or **Layout**.
3. Hover a button to read its name. Most of these buttons have no default key, so the hover is the quickest guide.

Tip: The buttons in these panels act on the note or beat under the edit cursor. Select the beats first if you want the change to cover several notes.

## Rhythm: tuplets and ties

The **Tuplets and ties** group has three tools.

- **N-tuplet** fits a chosen number of notes into the time of another number. A triplet is a three-note group, and this tool gives you other groupings too. Use it for a group of five or seven notes in a bar that normally holds four.
- **Tie note** joins the note on the cursor to the one before it, so the sound continues. Use it when you want to make the tie from the panel instead of from the keyboard.
- **Tie beat / chord** ties a whole beat, or every note of a chord, to the beat before it. Use it when a chord rings through into the next beat.

To write a triplet with the keyboard, press `/`. To choose a different group size, use **Choose tuplet ratio** from the **Duration** group, or **N-tuplet** here.

Note: A tie joins two notes of the same pitch. It does not change how many beats a bar holds.

## Rhythm: sounding pitch and duration

The **Sounding pitch and duration** group changes how a note is written compared with how it sounds.

- **Sound duration** sets how long the note sounds when the written note is shorter or longer than it plays. Use it when the note should sound for longer than its written value.
- **8va** marks the notes to sound one octave higher than written. Use it when a guitar part is written low but sounds high.
- **8vb** marks the notes to sound one octave lower than written. Use it for the opposite case.
- **15ma** marks the notes to sound two octaves higher than written.
- **15mb** marks the notes to sound two octaves lower than written.

Octave marks are for the reader. They show that a passage is written at a different pitch from the one you hear. Check the sound with `Space` after you add one.

Tip: Use the octave marks when a line is hard to read with many ledger lines above or below the staff. The mark tells the player where the notes really sound.

## Triplets and other rhythm keys

Several rhythm keys work straight from the keyboard. Use them while you write, and you will not need the panel.

- `/` toggles a triplet on the note under the cursor.
- `.` adds a dot, and a second press adds a second dot.
- `L` ties the note to the one before it.
- `R` turns the beat into a rest, and a second press turns it back.

These are explained in the chapter on writing notes, with the steps.

## Layout: voices

A voice is a separate line of notes that sits on the same staff. Voices let two parts of the music share one track. The **Voices** group has three tools.

- **Voice 1** switches the notes you write to the first voice. Use it for the main melody or the top part.
- **Voice 2** switches to the second voice. Use it for a bass line or a harmony under the melody.
- **Gray inactive voice** shows the other voice in grey. Use it to make the voice you are not writing easier to tell apart.

Note: Voice buttons change where new notes go. They do not change the notes already written.

## Layout: system breaks

A system is one line of the score. These tools control where a line ends.

- **Force line break** starts a new line at the bar under the cursor. Use it to make a section start on a new line.
- **Prevent line break** stops a line from ending at the bar under the cursor. Use it to keep a phrase together on one line.

Use the two tools together to shape the page. A forced break and a prevented break both only change the look of the page, not the sound.

## Layout: beaming

Beams join short notes together with a bar, so they are easy to read. These tools control the beams.

- **Automatic beaming** lets TabForge choose the beams for the bar. This is the normal setting. Use it when you want the score to look tidy without thinking about it.
- **Force beam group** joins the selected notes into one beam group. Use it when the automatic beams split a group you want together.
- **Break primary beam** cuts the main beam between the notes, so they read as separate notes. Use it when a beam joins notes that should stand apart.
- **Break secondary beam** cuts the second beam, the one for the shorter notes, so the 16th and 32nd notes read separately.

Tip: If the beams look wrong, press **Automatic beaming** first. Then use the break or force tools only where you need them.

## Layout: stems

A stem is the vertical line on a note. The stem can point up or down.

- **Automatic stem direction** sets the stem for you. This is the normal setting.
- **Invert stem direction** flips the stems of the selected notes. Use it when the notes would read better with the stem on the other side.

Warning: Inverting the stems changes only how the score looks. It does not change the notes or the sound.

## Zoom

Zoom makes the notes bigger or smaller on screen. It does not change the song.

- **Zoom box.** The **View and tempo** pane at the bottom of the side panel has a zoom box. Open it to choose a preset from 50 % to 200 %. **Fit width** is the first choice and the default. It fits the score to the width of the window.
- **Type a value.** In the zoom box you can type your own value from 50 to 200, then press `Enter`.
- **Zoom in and zoom out.** Press `Ctrl+=` to make the score larger, and `Ctrl+-` to make it smaller. The zoom buttons next to the zoom box do the same.
- **Zoom 75%, Zoom 100% and Zoom 150%.** These are in the **View** menu. Choose one to jump to that size.
- **Mouse wheel.** Hold `Ctrl` and turn the mouse wheel over the score to zoom in or out.

Tip: If the notes are too small to read, pick **Zoom 150%** from the **View** menu. If they are too big to see a whole line, choose **Fit width**.

Note: **Mouse-wheel scroll distance** in **Options > Preferences > Editing** sets how far one notch of the mouse wheel scrolls the score.

## Score and tab views

These options change what is shown on the score. They are in the **View** menu.

- **Tablature + standard** shows the tab and the staff together. This is the view you need to type frets.
- **Tablature only** hides the staff, so only the tab is shown.
- **Standard notation only** hides the tab. In this view a digit chooses a string, not a fret.
- **Dark score page** and **Light score page** change the colours of the page.
- **Horizontal score scrolling** switches the score between wrapped lines, which scroll down, and one line, which scrolls to the right.

Tip: If you press a digit and it changes the string instead of the fret, switch back to **Tablature + standard**.

## Show and hide panels

These options show or hide the parts of the window. Each can be turned back on.

- **Show / hide side panel** hides the tools, sections and practice panel, so the score has more room. Show it again to get the tools back.
- **Show / hide fretboard / keyboard** hides the instrument panel. Use it when you want more of the score on screen.
- **Show / hide Band view** switches the window to the Band layout, where each track has its instrument and tab in rows. Switch it again to return to your previous layout.
- **Arrangement overview** (`F8`) shows or hides the timeline at the bottom of the window.
- **Show track list** (`F3`) brings the mixer table of all tracks into view.
- **Compact mode** (`Ctrl+Alt+C`) hides every track row and shows the master track lane larger, so the score has more room. Press it again to bring the tracks back.
- **Fullscreen** (`F11`) fills the screen with the window. Press `F11` again to leave it.
- **Reset all panels to default positions** puts every panel back where it started.

## Follow and highlight during playback

These settings control what the score does while the song plays.

- **Follow playback on / off** (`Ctrl+Shift+F`) turns the score and timeline following the playhead on or off. When it is on, the score moves to keep the play position in view.
- **Highlight playing bar** shades the bar that is playing with a light band behind the notes. It is on by default.
- **Smooth page-turn follow** switches following between instant page turns and smooth, gliding ones.

Tip: If the score jumps around while you are trying to read it, turn off **Follow playback** with `Ctrl+Shift+F`.

## The tempo box

The tempo is the speed the song is written to play, in beats per minute.

1. Find the **BPM** box in the top toolbar.
2. Click it and type a number from 20 to 400.
3. Press `Enter`. The song now plays at that tempo.

Changing the BPM box changes the written tempo for the whole song. Use **Tempo change** in the **Structure** panel to change it from one bar onward. The structure chapter explains that tool.

## Playback speed

The **Speed** box changes how fast the song plays, without changing the written tempo. Notes keep their pitch.

- Find the **Speed** box in the **View and tempo** pane of the side panel, beside the tempo box.
- Open the list and choose a preset: 50 %, 75 %, 100 %, 125 %, 150 % or 200 %.
- Or click the box, type a percentage, and press `Enter`. Typing `75` means 75 % speed, not 75 beats per minute.
- Press `Ctrl+Alt+Up` to move to the next faster preset. Press `Ctrl+Alt+Down` to move to the next slower one.
- The command **Reset speed to 100 %** puts the song back at its own tempo.

Tip: To learn a hard passage, set the Speed box to 50 %. Once it is easy, move up one preset at a time.

Note: The Speed box and the BPM box are different. The Speed box only changes playback. The BPM box changes the song.

## Playback keys

These keys control the play position while you check your work.

- `Space` plays from the cursor, and pauses if the song is already playing.
- `Shift+Space` plays from the start of the song.
- `F9` turns the loop on. A loop repeats the selected bars, or the loop area you chose.

Tip: Select a few bars, then press `F9` and `Space` to hear only those bars over and over.

## Common questions

Question: What is the difference between the BPM box and the Speed box?
Answer: The BPM box sets the written tempo of the song. The Speed box changes how fast it plays, without changing the tempo written in the score.

Question: Why do my notes look too small on the screen?
Answer: The zoom is set low. Choose **Fit width** or **Zoom 150%** from the zoom box or the **View** menu, or hold `Ctrl` and turn the mouse wheel.

Question: What does Force line break do?
Answer: It starts a new line of the score at the bar under the cursor. It only changes how the page looks.

Question: Does changing the stems change the sound?
Answer: No. Stems only change how the score looks. The notes and the sound stay the same.

Question: How do I get the score back to a clean layout?
Answer: Choose **Reset all panels to default positions** from the **View** menu. Then check the zoom box and choose **Fit width**.

## Troubleshooting

Problem: The score is too small to read.
Fix: Open the zoom box in the **View and tempo** pane and choose **Zoom 150%** or type a larger value. You can also hold `Ctrl` and turn the mouse wheel up.

Problem: The score is too big, and I can only see a few notes.
Fix: Open the zoom box and choose **Fit width**, or type a smaller value such as 75.

Problem: The song plays too slowly, and I do not know why.
Fix: The Speed box is below 100 %. Open the Speed box in the **View and tempo** pane and choose 100 %. Check the BPM box too.

Problem: I changed the BPM, but the song still plays at the old speed.
Fix: The Speed box may be below 100 % so it multiplies the tempo. Set the Speed box to 100 %, then check the BPM again.

Problem: The score keeps moving while I try to read a bar.
Fix: **Follow playback** is on. Press `Ctrl+Shift+F` to turn it off while you read, then turn it on again when you want the score to follow the music.

Problem: The beams in a bar look wrong.
Fix: Choose **Automatic beaming** in the **Beaming** group of the **Layout** panel. Then use **Force beam group** or a break tool only where the automatic beam does not suit the music.

Problem: The stems point the wrong way.
Fix: Choose **Automatic stem direction** to let the score set them. Or choose **Invert stem direction** for the notes that need flipping.

Problem: A line of the score starts in the wrong place.
Fix: A forced line break or a prevented line break may be in the way. Move the cursor to the bar and check the **System layout** buttons. The break only changes the page, so the notes are fine.

Problem: The side panel has disappeared, and I cannot find the tools.
Fix: Press the toolbar button that shows and hides the side panel, or choose **View > Panels**. You can also choose **View > Reset all panels to default positions**.

Problem: The score is in one long line, and I want it to wrap.
Fix: Turn off **Horizontal score scrolling** in the **View** menu. The score returns to wrapped lines.

Problem: I pressed `Ctrl+=` and the score did not get bigger.
Fix: Click the score first, so the score has the keyboard focus, then press `Ctrl+=` again. A text box that has the focus takes the keys instead.
