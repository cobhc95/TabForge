# Welcome to TabForge

> **Note:** The Detailed Guide covers everything; the Basic Guide covers the essentials. Both are kept up to date with the program.

Welcome to TabForge. It is a Windows program for reading, playing and writing music for guitar, bass, keys and drums. You see the music as tablature (TAB), which shows which fret to press on which string, and as standard notation. When you press play, you hear it.

You do not need to read standard notation, and you do not need to have used a tab editor before. This guide starts with opening a song and pressing play, and ends with a finished song you have saved, exported and shared.

## What you will learn

- What TabForge does and what you can build with it.
- How this guide is organised, and how to find what you need.
- How to read the conventions used in the text.
- How to open the demo song and hear it play.

## What TabForge does

TabForge keeps everything in one window: the song, the instrument, the timeline and the controls. Here is what you can do with it.

- **Play and practise.** Open a song, slow it down, loop a hard bar and play along with a click.
- **Write.** Type frets with the keyboard or click them on the fretboard. Add rests, chords and techniques such as bends and palm mutes.
- **Build a band.** Add bass, drums and keys, set tunings and a capo, and balance the mix.
- **Arrange.** Lay out sections such as Intro, Verse and Chorus, and move them around.
- **Share.** Save your song, export a PDF, and render the song to an audio file.

## Who this guide is for

This guide is for beginners. You might play guitar and want to learn a song at your own speed. You might have a riff in your head and want to write it down. You might want a quick demo of an idea. All three are covered, and you can read the chapters in the order that suits you.

## How to use the guide

Open the tutorial any time from **Help > Tutorial…**. It starts with the Basic Guide, which covers the essentials. This Detailed Guide is the full reference, and it can also be read as a PDF. Use the contents list to jump to the chapter you need.

Every chapter has hands-on exercises headed **Try it**. They use two things you can always get back to:

- **Ashen Meridian**, an original demo song written for TabForge. It comes with the program, in the `Samples` folder beside `TabForge.exe`.
- **My first riff**, a tiny four-bar riff you write yourself in Chapter 5 and add to as you go.

> **Tip:** Chapter 14 lists common problems in the words people use to describe them, such as "there is no sound". If something goes wrong, start there.

## How to read the text

A few simple habits keep the instructions short.

- Names you see on screen are in **bold**: click **Play / pause**, open **Track properties**.
- Menu paths join the menu and the item with a greater-than sign: **File > Open…**.
- Keyboard shortcuts follow the action, in brackets: Save (`Ctrl+S`). Join keys with a plus sign, so `Ctrl+Shift+A` means hold all three and press `A`.
- Each step is one action. If a step changes what you see, the next sentence says what you should now see.

You will also meet three kinds of note.

> **Tip:** A tip is a shortcut or a better way. You can skip it and still succeed.

Notes explain something that might surprise you.

> **Note:** The shortcuts in this guide are the TabForge default ones. If you change them in **Preferences**, the keys on your own computer will differ from the ones printed here.

A warning appears only when you could lose work or hear something very loud.

> **Warning:** TabForge is a pre-release. Most things work well, but keep a backup of any song you care about.

## A first listen

*Goal: hear the demo song and watch it play.*

Before you start, turn your computer's volume down a little. The demo song is lively.

### Try it: Play Ashen Meridian

1. Choose **File > Open…** (`Ctrl+O`).
2. In the window that opens, go to the `Samples` folder beside `TabForge.exe`.
3. Choose `TabForge Demo - Ashen Meridian.gp`, then click **Open**.
4. Wait a moment. The song opens, and its title appears in the tab at the top of the window.
5. Press `Space`. The song starts, and a line moves across the score as the music plays.
6. Press `Space` again to pause.

You have now opened a real song and heard it. The notes on screen are exactly the ones you heard.

> **Note:** Ashen Meridian is a demo song, built to show everything TabForge can do, so it is fast and busy. Chapter 3 shows how to slow it down.

If you heard nothing, do not worry. Sound problems are common and nearly always easy to fix. See **Chapter 14: Troubleshooting and FAQ**.
