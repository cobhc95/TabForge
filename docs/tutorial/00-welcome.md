# Welcome to TabForge

Welcome to TabForge. It is a tab editor: a program for reading, playing and writing music for guitar, bass, keys and drums. You see your music as tablature (TAB) and as standard notation, and you hear it played back as you work.

This guide is short on purpose. It covers the basics that rarely change, so that you can start quickly and find the rest by exploring. Each chapter takes about ten minutes.

## What you will learn

- Say what TabForge is for.
- Open this guide and search it.
- Know which chapter to read first.
- Find help inside the program when the guide does not cover something.

## What TabForge does

You open a song, press play and follow the notes on the screen. You can slow a hard part down, repeat it and play along with a click. You can also write your own music by typing fret numbers, add more tracks, balance their volume and save the result or send it to someone else.

TabForge opens `.gp`, `.gpx`, `.gp5`, `.gp4`, `.gp3` and `.tforge` song files. It saves `.gp` and `.tforge` files, and it can export a song as MIDI, PDF, MusicXML, text tab or an audio file.

A demo song comes with the program. It sits in a folder called `Samples` beside `TabForge.exe`, and it is the song this guide uses for its exercises. Opening it is safe: an opened song is a copy in memory, and the file on disk changes only if you save over it.

## Open and search this guide

Choose **Help > Tutorial…** to open the guide in its own window. The chapters are listed in order. Click one to read it, or use the search box at the top to look across every chapter at once. Type a word such as `loop` or `mute`, and the matching chapters and headings appear. The window also has a button to export the guide as a PDF.

> **Tip:** The guide stays open beside the main window, so you can read a step, switch to TabForge, and come back.

For the full reference, open the Detailed Guide from the Tutorial window.

## How the guide is written

Names that you see on screen are in bold, such as **File > Open…**. Things you type, and keys, are in code style, such as `Space`. When a command has a keyboard shortcut, you can always read it by hovering the mouse over the control or looking beside the command in its menu. This guide prints only a handful of keys that are very unlikely to change. If you change a key yourself, the key you set is the one that works.

Each chapter has a short exercise marked **Try it**. Do these on the demo song or on a new song. None of them needs you to save anything.

> **Note:** TabForge is still being developed, so a label or a menu may differ slightly from this guide. The names of the menus and the actions stay the same, and the menus themselves are the most reliable map.

## The chapters

- **Chapter 1: Getting started** opens a song, plays it and moves around.
- **Chapter 2: Practising** covers looping, slowing down, the metronome and the count-in.
- **Chapter 3: Reading and writing tab** explains how fret numbers work and how to write notes.
- **Chapter 4: Tracks and sound** adds tracks, picks instruments and sets volume.
- **Chapter 5: Saving and sharing** saves your work and exports it.
- **Chapter 6: Troubleshooting and help** fixes the common problems.

If you only want to listen and practise, read chapters 1 and 2. If you want to write music, add chapter 3.

## Quick recap

- TabForge is a tab editor that plays your music as you read and write it.
- **Help > Tutorial…** opens this guide, and its search box looks through every chapter.
- The demo song is in the `Samples` folder beside `TabForge.exe`.
- Hover any control to see its name and its shortcut.
